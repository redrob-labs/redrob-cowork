**Copyright Act, 1957 के अंश**

> धारा 17: "Subject to the provisions of this Act, the author of a work shall be the first owner of the copyright therein: Provided that— … (c) in the case of a work made in the course of the author's employment under a contract of service or apprenticeship, … the employer shall, in the absence of any agreement to the contrary, be the first owner of the copyright therein." (परंतुक (a) और (b) केवल पत्र-पत्रिकाओं की रचनाओं तथा मूल्य पर बनवाए गए photograph, painting, portrait, engraving या cinematograph film पर लागू हैं।)
>
> धारा 19 (संक्षिप्त): (1) किसी कृति में copyright का assignment तभी वैध है जब वह लिखित हो और assignor या उसके विधिवत अधिकृत agent द्वारा हस्ताक्षरित हो। (2)–(3) assignment में कृति की पहचान होगी, assign किए गए अधिकार (देय royalty, यदि कोई हो, सहित) बताए जाएँगे, तथा assignment की अवधि और क्षेत्रीय विस्तार भी। (4) यदि assignee एक वर्ष के भीतर अधिकारों का प्रयोग नहीं करता, तो assignment समाप्त माना जाएगा, जब तक assignment में अन्यथा उल्लेख न हो। (5) अवधि का उल्लेख न हो तो अवधि पाँच वर्ष मानी जाएगी। (6) क्षेत्रीय विस्तार का उल्लेख न हो तो वह भारत माना जाएगा।

**DPDP Act, 2023 के अंश (संक्षिप्त)**

> धारा 8(2): Data Fiduciary अपनी ओर से personal data के processing के लिए Data Processor को केवल valid contract के तहत लगा सकता है। धारा 8(5): Data Fiduciary, Data Processor द्वारा किए गए processing सहित, personal data breach रोकने के लिए reasonable security safeguards रखेगा। धारा 16(1): केंद्र सरकार notification द्वारा जिन देशों को प्रतिबंधित करे, उनके अतिरिक्त भारत के बाहर transfer की अनुमति है।

## A strong answer

- Notices that the milestones add up to ₹1.96 crore, not the ₹2 crore fixed fee (a ₹4 lakh gap). Resolves the gap with a placeholder or a stated assumption and raises it in the cover email instead of silently picking one figure.
- Doesn't rely on "work for hire": Indian law has no such concept for a commissioned work, and under section 17 an independent agency (a contract for services, not of service) is the first owner. Includes a present assignment ("hereby assigns") in a signed writing that satisfies section 19: identifies the works and rights, states the term as the full copyright term and the territory as worldwide, and disapplies the one-year lapse in section 19(4); adds a waiver/non-assertion of moral rights to the extent permitted, plus further-assurances language.
- Carves out Orbitmade Core with a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, worldwide license that is transferable with the app. Restricts open source to pre-approved permissive licenses and prohibits GPL-3.0 components in the distributed app (or requires Fernhill's written approval), explaining the copyleft risk to the source code.
- Balances acceptance (for example 10 business days, a written rejection with reasons, two cure rounds, and deemed acceptance only after a reminder notice) and the liability cap (for example 12 months of fees or the full contract value, with exclusions for IP indemnity, confidentiality/personal-data breach and wilful misconduct). Explains both in the cover email.
- Makes the agreement the "valid contract" required by DPDP s.8(2): processing only on Fernhill's instructions, security safeguards, breach notice to Fernhill without delay so it can notify the Board and users, deletion on exit. Requires prior written approval of subcontractors and their countries (none in a restricted country under s.16), flow-down of obligations, and Orbitmade's responsibility for its subcontractors. On termination, Fernhill receives the source code and work in progress on payment of amounts due. Indian law governs, with a stated Mumbai seat (courts or arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996). Keeps the non-solicit narrow, noting its enforceability after the term is uncertain under section 27 of the Contract Act.
