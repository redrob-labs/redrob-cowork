## A strong answer

- Catches that the 2023 취업규칙 §7.2 is superseded by PB-2025-04: she is eligible for 16 weeks of company-paid leave at 100% because she will have well over 6 months of service when leave starts (about 11.5 months). Does not tell her to rely on 연차 and unpaid leave; also explains that 출산전후휴가 (90 days) has no service requirement at all.
- Gets the 연차 arithmetic right under 제60조②: one day per completed month of attendance, capped at 11 in the first year. Today she has 8 accrued (Mar 3–Nov 2) − 4 used = 4 days; when her 11th month completes on Feb 2, 2026 she reaches the 11-day cap, so at most 11 − 4 = 7 days. She cannot save 10. These days must be used by Mar 2, 2026 (one year from hire) or they lapse (paid out as 연차수당 if the company has not run 사용 촉진), so in practice she should take them before her 출산전후휴가 starts. Notes that leave counts as attendance, so 15 new days arise on Mar 3, 2026.
- Explains that she cannot change the 보육지원 allocation now: the birth is the qualifying event, so she has 30 days after it (about Mar 18, 2026, if the baby arrives on the due date). The ₩3,000,000 limit is per household.
- On job protection: 출산전후휴가 is protected by statute (same or equivalent job on return under 제74조⑥, no dismissal during the leave and 30 days after under 제23조②). She will also meet the 6-month test for 육아휴직, which is protected under 남녀고용평등법 §19. The company leave runs concurrently with the 90 days and is topped up to 100%.
- Treats manager notice as a judgement call: 30 days' notice means telling her manager by mid-January (about Jan 16) for a Feb 16 start, so her January timing works, and HR keeps the matter confidential until then.
- The internal note flags that the superseded 취업규칙 is still on the intranet (and that the change should be reflected in the 취업규칙 itself and filed under 근로기준법 §93). It also flags that PB-2025-04 doesn't say whether weeks 13–16 (after the 90-day 출산전후휴가 ends) count as 육아휴직 or are otherwise job-protected, and routes that to 법무/노무 instead of promising protection.
