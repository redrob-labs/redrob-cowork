## Prompt

You are an enterprise AE at Parcelwise, which sells returns-management software for e-commerce brands: a branded returns portal, exchange-first flows and warehouse grading. You're preparing to re-open **Ridgeback Outfitters**, a US direct-to-consumer outdoor apparel brand. Here is what the research turned up, in no particular order.

**Search results and sources**

1. *Ridgeback press release, 14 Jan 2026:* "Ridgeback Outfitters names Jane Doe Chief Executive Officer. Doe, previously CFO, succeeds founder Richard Roe, who becomes Executive Chair. FY2025 net revenue grew 18% to $240 million."
2. *Retail blog, June 2023:* "Ridgeback CEO Richard Roe on hitting $180M and why they built their returns portal in-house."
3. *Podcast transcript, Nov 2025, John Stiles (COO, Ridgeback):* "Our return rate is around 22%. All-in, a return costs us about $14 by the time it's back on the shelf. Average order is about $96. Exchanges are a manual email today."
4. *Industry report, 2025:* "Apparel e-commerce return rates average 32%."
5. *Companies registry snippet:* "Ridgeback Outfitters Ltd, a camping equipment retailer in Leeds, UK, incorporated 2009."
6. *Ridgeback careers page, posted 2 weeks ago:* "Returns Operations Manager: own reverse logistics end to end; evaluate tools to replace our homegrown returns portal; reports to the COO."
7. *Professional profile:* Mary Major, VP E-commerce at Ridgeback since Sep 2025. Previously Director of Digital at Tamarack Goods (a Parcelwise customer since 2023).
8. *Ridgeback blog, Feb 2026:* "We're launching a resale programme for used gear this spring."

**Parcelwise CRM**

- Opportunity "Ridgeback – Returns Portal", Closed Lost, Aug 2024. Reason: "No budget; happy with in-house tool."
- Champion: Richard Miles, Director of CX. Note: "Left Ridgeback in 2025."

**Parcelwise proof points (from our own case studies)**

- Customers typically cut return-processing cost by 20–30%.
- Exchange-first flows convert about 15% of refunds into exchanges.
- Native grading workflows for resale and second-hand programmes.

**Deliver** (markdown, at most ~1,200 words) an account research brief with:

1. A company snapshot: the current facts only, each with its source number. Separate the facts you trust from those you're discarding, and say why.
2. "Why now": the triggers, ranked.
3. The buying committee: likely roles, the entry point you recommend, and how to handle the previous loss.
4. A value hypothesis with the calculation shown. Label clearly which inputs are Ridgeback's own and which are Parcelwise benchmarks.
5. Risks and unknowns.
6. Your recommended first move (channel, person, message angle) and five discovery questions.

## A strong answer

- Uses the current leadership (Jane Doe as CEO since Jan 2026; Richard Roe as Executive Chair) and the FY2025 revenue of $240M. Treats the 2023 article (Roe as CEO, $180M) as outdated context only.
- Discards source 5 (a different UK company) and doesn't use the 32% industry average in place of Ridgeback's own 22% return rate.
- The value math is labelled and correct: $240M ÷ $96 ≈ 2.5M orders, × 22% ≈ 550,000 returns, × $14 ≈ $7.7M a year in processing cost. Parcelwise's 20–30% benchmark gives about $1.5–2.3M of potential savings. These are presented as estimates to validate, and the 15% exchange conversion is noted as upside.
- Uses the CRM history well: the 2024 loss reason was the in-house tool and budget, and the champion has left. The new triggers that change the situation are the job posting to replace the homegrown portal, the new CEO and the resale launch. The recommended entry is John Stiles (the COO, who owns the new hire) and/or Mary Major (via the Tamarack Goods connection), with a justification.
- Gives a concrete first move and discovery questions that test the assumptions (actual cost per return, the exchange process, the evaluation timeline, resale grading needs).
