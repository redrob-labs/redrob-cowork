## Prompt

You are the Head of People at Lumen Ridge Software, Inc. (340 employees). The CEO wants a single **Hybrid and Remote Work Policy** for the handbook, replacing three conflicting memos. Write the policy.

**Headcount by state**

| State | Employees | Of which non-exempt | Office? |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | 120 | 18 | San Jose office |
| Illinois | 70 | 12 | Chicago office |
| Texas | 85 | 20 | Austin HQ |
| New York | 40 | 5 | None (fully remote) |
| Washington | 25 | 3 | None (fully remote) |

**CEO's instructions (email)**

> 1. Everyone near an office comes in 3 days a week (Tue–Thu). Everyone else is remote.
> 2. Replace all expense claims with a flat $50/month home-office stipend for everyone. No more receipts.
> 3. People can "work from anywhere" for up to 4 weeks a year, including abroad.
> 4. Non-exempt staff shouldn't be paid for logging in to the VPN or booting up before their shift. That's not work.
> 5. Keep it short and friendly.

**Existing memos (excerpts)**

> Memo A (2023): "Employees must live and work in their state of hire. Moving to another state requires People team approval at least 60 days in advance."
> Memo B (2024): "Remote employees within 50 miles of an office are expected in the office twice a week."
> Memo C (2024): "Phone and internet are reimbursed at 50% of the monthly bill, up to $60, with a receipt."

**Research notes from employment counsel (provided to you; treat as accurate)**

> California Labor Code §2802(a): "An employer shall indemnify his or her employee for all necessary expenditures or losses incurred by the employee in direct consequence of the discharge of his or her duties…" Counsel's note: a flat stipend is acceptable only if it covers actual necessary expenses. Employees must be able to claim the shortfall.
>
> Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act, 820 ILCS 115/9.5 (abridged): employers must reimburse employees for all necessary expenditures within the scope of employment and directly related to services performed for the employer. An employer may set a written reimbursement policy, including specifications or guidelines for submission of documentation, and is not liable if the employee fails to comply with it.
>
> Federal wage-hour guidance (counsel's summary): for non-exempt employees, time spent on activities that are integral and indispensable to the job, such as booting up and logging in to required systems, is generally compensable work time.
>
> Working abroad (counsel's summary): even short stays can create immigration, tax-withholding and corporate permanent-establishment risk. Any overseas work needs pre-approval, and counsel recommends limiting it to countries where the company already has an entity or employer-of-record (currently Canada and the UK).

**Deliver** the policy in markdown with these headed sections: Purpose and scope; Work arrangements (onsite / hybrid / remote, and who falls into which); Office attendance; Working from another location (in the US and abroad); Changing your home state; Equipment and expense reimbursement (with state-specific rules); Hours, timekeeping and non-exempt employees; Exceptions and accommodations; Policy owner and effective date. Add a short table showing what applies by state.

After the policy, add a note to the CEO of at most 200 words. It should explain where you departed from the instructions, why, and what decision you need from them. Keep the whole answer under about 1,200 words, in plain, friendly language that still binds.

## A strong answer

- Resolves the three conflicting memos into one rule set: the attendance rule (3 days vs. twice a week, and who counts as "near" an office), keeping the state-move approval, and expressly superseding Memos A–C.
- Keeps the $50 stipend but adds a claims route for necessary expenses above it in California and Illinois, consistent with §2802 and 820 ILCS 115/9.5. Does not adopt "no more receipts" as written, and states a documentation requirement.
- Rejects instruction 4. Boot-up and VPN login time for non-exempt employees is treated as paid work, with a clear timekeeping rule.
- Narrows "work from anywhere": US locations allowed within limits; overseas only with pre-approval and only in Canada or the UK, for the immigration, tax and permanent-establishment reasons given.
- The note to the CEO lists each departure (stipend, VPN time, abroad, attendance radius) and the decision needed. The by-state table matches the headcount data, for example that New York and Washington have no office.
