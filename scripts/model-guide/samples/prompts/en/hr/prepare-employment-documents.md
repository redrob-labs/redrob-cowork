## Prompt

You are the HR business partner at Brightwater Analytics, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Austin, Texas. The recruiter has asked you to "turn this into the offer letter today" for a remote engineer based in California. Prepare the offer letter and a cover note to the hiring manager. Today is November 10, 2025.

**Approved requisition REQ-2291 (signed by VP Engineering and Finance)**

| Field | Approved value |
|---|---|
| Title / level | Software Engineer II (L4) |
| Base salary band (L4, US remote) | $148,000 – $175,000 |
| Equity | 3,200 RSUs, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (approved by the Compensation Committee on Oct 28) |
| Signing bonus | Up to $20,000 |
| Classification | Full-time, exempt |
| Reports to | Mary Major, Engineering Manager |

**Recruiter's notes (verbal offer accepted by the candidate on Nov 7)**

> Candidate: John Stiles, San Diego, CA. Told him "Senior Software Engineer", base $182,000, 4,000 RSUs, $20k signing bonus, start Monday Dec 1. He asked us to drop the non-compete. I said it's standard. Please use our usual template.

**Usual template, relevant excerpts**

> **4. Signing bonus.** You will receive a one-time signing bonus of $[X], payable in your first regular paycheck. If you resign voluntarily within 12 months of your start date, you must repay the full bonus.
>
> **7. Non-competition.** For 12 months after your employment ends, you will not work for or provide services to any competitor of the Company in the United States.
>
> **9. Governing law and venue.** This letter is governed by Texas law, and any dispute will be resolved exclusively in the state or federal courts in Travis County, Texas.
>
> **10. At-will employment.** Your employment is at will. Either you or the Company may end it at any time, with or without cause or notice.

**Legal's guidance notes (excerpts provided to HR)**

> California Business and Professions Code §16600(a): "Except as provided in this chapter, every contract by which anyone is restrained from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void."
>
> California Labor Code §925(a) (abridged): an employer shall not require an employee who primarily resides and works in California, as a condition of employment, to agree to a provision that would require the employee to adjudicate outside California a claim arising in California, or deprive the employee of the substantive protection of California law for a controversy arising in California.
>
> Company practice: signing-bonus repayment for California hires is pro-rated by full months worked, and is never deducted from final wages without separate written authorization.

**Deliverables (markdown):**

1. **The offer letter**, ready to send, with headed sections: position, compensation, equity, signing bonus, start date, benefits (one sentence; refer to the benefits guide), contingencies (background check, proof of work authorization), at-will employment, governing law, and acceptance. Use only terms you are authorized to offer. Where a term depends on a decision still open, put a bracketed placeholder and explain it in the cover note.
2. **A cover note to Mary Major** (at most 300 words). It should list each mismatch between what was promised and what is approved, recommend how to resolve each one, and say who has to sign off.
3. **A worked example of the signing-bonus repayment**: what John would owe if he resigned after 7 full months.

Keep the whole answer under about 1,200 words.

## A strong answer

- Does not put $182,000, 4,000 RSUs or "Senior" into the letter. These exceed the approved L4 band, the Compensation Committee grant and the requisition title. Uses $175,000 (or a placeholder), 3,200 RSUs and "Software Engineer II", and flags each one for approval.
- Takes a clear position on the base-pay gap: either ask for a band exception with Finance and VP Engineering sign-off, or bridge it within approved limits. Explains why, and notes that the candidate must be told before the letter goes out, because he accepted different terms verbally.
- Drops the non-compete under §16600 instead of keeping it. Replaces Texas law and Travis County venue with California law and venue (or removes the clause) under Labor Code §925, and keeps a confidentiality/IP reference.
- Pro-rates the signing bonus by full months: after 7 months he would owe 5/12 × $20,000 = $8,333.33. States that it won't be deducted from final pay without separate written authorization.
- Keeps the at-will statement, the contingencies and the Dec 1, 2025 start date. The letter reads as a finished document, with placeholders limited to the open decisions.
