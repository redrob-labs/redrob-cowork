## Prompt

You are the content lead at Corbel Climate Co. Thermalyn Pro, a smart thermostat for small businesses (cafés, salons, retail shops), launches on December 1, 2025. Write the launch creative from the brief and data below.

**Brief from product marketing**

> Headline idea: "Cut your energy bills by 40%." Price: "$149." Audience: owners of 1–3 locations who pay the bills themselves and hate fiddling with thermostats. Key benefits: schedules learn your opening hours; staff can't override beyond ±2°F; alerts when a door is left open with the HVAC running.

**Pilot results (48 small-business sites, Jun–Sep 2025)**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Median reduction in HVAC energy use | 18% |
| Top-quartile reduction in HVAC energy use | 27% |
| Best single site | 41% |
| HVAC share of a typical pilot site's total electricity bill | 40% |
| Sites saying "easier than our old thermostat" | 44 of 48 |

Customer quote (approved for use): *"Our summer bill dropped by about $210 a month, and nobody fights over the thermostat anymore."* (Jane Doe, owner, Doe & Daughters Bakery)

**Price sheet (finance)**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Thermalyn Pro device, list | $179 |
| Launch price (through Jan 31, 2026) | $149 |
| Corbel Cloud subscription (required for schedules and alerts) | $8 per location per month |

**Brand and legal rules**

- Voice: plain, confident, a little dry. No superlatives ("best", "smartest", "#1"). Sentence case.
- Colours: Deep Spruce `#1F4D3A`, Signal Amber `#F2A93B`, Paper `#F7F5F0`. Typeface: Inter.
- Any savings claim needs the footnote "Based on a 2025 pilot of 48 small-business sites. Results vary." and must say what is being reduced (HVAC energy or the total bill).
- Any price shown must include the subscription where the feature depends on it.

**Assets needed**

| Asset | Limits |
|---|---|
| Landing-page hero | H1 of 8 words or fewer; subhead of 25 words or fewer; one CTA button; a proof line |
| Meta feed ad (1080×1080) | Headline of 40 characters or fewer; primary text of 125 characters or fewer; on-image text of 6 words or fewer |
| Google responsive search ad | 3 headlines of 30 characters or fewer each; 2 descriptions of 90 characters or fewer each |

**Deliver**, in markdown:

1. The copy for each asset, with a character or word count next to each line.
2. Layout and visual direction for the hero and the Meta ad: hierarchy, imagery, colour use, and where the footnote sits.
3. An inline SVG mock-up of the landing-page hero (1200×630) in a code block, using the brand colours and real copy.
4. A short note to product marketing (at most 120 words) explaining any changes you made to the brief and why.

Keep the written parts under about 1,200 words. The SVG doesn't count toward the limit.

## A strong answer

- Doesn't lead with "Cut your energy bills by 40%". That is the single best site, and it measures HVAC energy, not the bill. Uses a defensible claim instead, such as a median of 18% less HVAC energy (about 7% of a typical total bill), with the required footnote.
- Shows price honestly: $149 as a launch price, with list $179 and the end date, plus $8/month per location, since schedules and alerts depend on the subscription.
- Every line respects its limit, and the counts shown are accurate. No superlatives; sentence case throughout.
- The SVG is valid and self-contained, uses `#1F4D3A`, `#F2A93B` and `#F7F5F0`, and carries the H1, subhead, CTA and footnote, with a clear hierarchy.
- The note to product marketing explains the claim and price changes in terms of the pilot data and the legal rules, and points to the quote and the 44-of-48 ease result as stronger proof.
