## Prompt

You do the books for Greenward Landscaping, a small crew owned by Richard Miles. It's **Wednesday 1 April 2026**. Richard wants three things: the March bank account reconciled, a clean March summary, and an answer to his question: "Can I pay cash for an $18,000 ride-on mower on 15 April?"

**March bank statement (opening balance 1 Mar: $24,200)**

| 03-03 | Pumpline Fuel | −612 |
| 03-05 | Payroll | −9,400 |
| 03-06 | Line of credit draw | +10,000 |
| 03-09 | Nursery Supply Co | −3,150 |
| 03-10 | Nursery Supply Co | −3,150 |
| 03-12 | Deposit: Maple Court Apartments, inv 2034 | +6,360 |
| 03-15 | Equipment lease | −1,100 |
| 03-19 | Payroll | −9,400 |

| | **Closing balance per bank** | **20,301** |

**Bookkeeping app:** cash balance at 31 Mar is **$21,686**. The app has one Nursery Supply bill for $3,150 (Nursery confirms there was only one invoice), no bank fee, and check #448 to Stonecraft Pavers for $1,800, written 30 Mar and not yet cleared. Streamflix is coded to "Software". Richard says it's his personal account.

**Invoice notes:** inv 2034 was $6,000 of work plus 6% sales tax ($360), due to the state on 20 April. Line of credit: $25,000 limit, 9% interest, interest-only, charged on the 1st of each month.

**Open receivables**

| Invoice | Client | Amount ($) | Due | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2029 | Birchwood Dental | 3,900 | 20 Feb | Disputed: "work incomplete" |
| 2035 | Cobble Hill School | 7,200 | 10 Apr | Always pays on time |
| 2037 | Hollis HOA | 4,800 | 15 Apr | Usually pays about 10 days late |

**Known April–May cash items**

- Payroll every other Thursday: 2 Apr $9,400. From 16 Apr it's **$11,200**, because two seasonal hires start.
- Fuel about $325 a week. Equipment lease $1,100 on the 15th. Insurance $840 on the 26th.
- Spring plant order: $2,500 on 10 Apr.
- Residential maintenance card receipts: about $2,500 a week, starting Fri 10 Apr.
- Spring clean-up jobs: $16,500 invoiced 30 Apr, net 15, so cash expected 15 May.
- Nursery Supply says a duplicate refund takes "5–10 business days once requested". It hasn't been requested yet.

**Deliver** (markdown, at most ~1,200 words):

1. A bank reconciliation table, from the bank balance to the book balance, listing every reconciling item. Include the corrections the books need.
2. A March summary: revenue, cost categories, and the items that are *not* revenue or business expense, with the reason for each.
3. An 8-week cash forecast in weekly columns, weeks starting Wed 1 Apr: opening balance, inflows, outflows, closing balance. Run it with and without the mower. State your assumptions and what's excluded.
4. A recommendation (at most 200 words) to Richard about the mower, with alternatives.

## A strong answer

- Reconciles the $1,385 difference ($21,686 − $20,301) as the duplicate Nursery charge (+$3,150, to be recovered), the unrecorded bank fee (+$35) and outstanding check #448 (−$1,800). Recodes Streamflix as an owner draw, not Software.
- March revenue is $13,450 ($4,800 + $6,000 + $2,650). The $10,000 line-of-credit draw is a liability and the $360 sales tax is a payable; neither is revenue.
- The forecast excludes the disputed Birchwood invoice (or shows it as an upside case) and the unconfirmed Nursery refund, applies the $11,200 payroll from 16 Apr, and puts Hollis about 10 days late. Without the mower, cash bottoms out at about $2,100 in the week of 29 Apr and ends near $12,000.
- With an $18,000 cash purchase on 15 Apr, the balance goes deeply negative (about −$15,900 in the week of 29 Apr). Even the remaining $15,000 of credit line wouldn't fully cover it. The recommendation is not to pay cash, and suggests equipment financing, deferring, or leasing.
- Every computed column states its method, and the arithmetic is consistent week to week.
