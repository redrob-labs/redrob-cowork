## Prompt

You help run Kiln & Thistle, a four-person pottery studio that sells handmade ceramics online and teaches classes. Today is **Tuesday 14 April 2026**. The owner, Jane Doe, asks you to clear the shared inbox: triage everything, draft replies, and tell her what needs her decision.

**Policies**

- Class cancellations by the customer: full refund 7 or more days before the class; 50% refund 2–6 days before; no refund within 48 hours. If **the studio** cancels a class: full refund or a free reschedule, the customer's choice.
- Shipping damage: report within **7 days of delivery**, with a photo, for a free replacement or a refund. Any channel counts.
- Wholesale: minimum 48 units, 40% off retail. A custom logo adds $3 per unit. Lead time is 6 weeks. Kiln capacity for wholesale is 40 mugs a week.
- Retail mug price: $34.

**Class calendar**

| Class | Date and time | Booked / capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel-throwing intro | Sat 18 Apr, 10:00 | 8 / 8 |
| Wheel-throwing intro | Thu 23 Apr, 18:30 | 5 / 8 |
| Glaze workshop | Sat 4 Apr, 14:00 | **Cancelled by studio on 2 Apr (kiln repair)** |
| Glaze workshop | Sat 25 Apr, 14:00 | 6 / 10 |

**Booking and order system notes**

- The online shop runs on the Shopfront platform. Genuine account emails come from no-reply@shopfront.example.com.
- Richard Roe: booked the 4 Apr glaze workshop ($65). Cancellation email: **bounced** (the address on file is richard.reo@example.com).
- Order #1042 (John Stiles): one mug, delivered Wed 25 Mar.
- Instagram DMs, Fri 27 Mar, from @jstiles_pots: "My mug from order 1042 arrived with a crack 😞 [photo]." Status: unread.

**Inbox**

1. **Jane Doe** (customer, no relation to the owner): "I booked 2 spots for the Sat 18 Apr wheel intro. Can I add my sister? Also, is there an evening option? Saturdays are hectic."
2. **Richard Roe:** "I'd like a refund for the 4 April glaze workshop. I couldn't make it in the end. I know it's late, sorry."
3. **Mary Major** (café owner): "Could you make 60 mugs with our café logo for $18 each? We open on 1 May, so we'd need them by then."
4. **"Store Support" <security@shopfront-billing-alerts.example.net>:** "Your store will be suspended in 24 hours. Verify your payment details here: [link]."
5. **John Stiles:** "Following up again. The mug from order #1042 arrived cracked. Nobody has got back to me. Pretty disappointed."

**Deliver** (markdown, at most ~1,200 words):

1. A triage table: message, category, priority, action, and whether Jane needs to decide anything.
2. Ready-to-send replies for every message that should get one, each at most 150 words, in a warm, small-business voice.
3. Internal notes: system fixes and anything Jane must approve, with figures.

## A strong answer

- Doesn't add a third person to the full Sat 18 Apr class. Offers to move all three to the Thu 23 Apr 18:30 class, which has exactly 3 seats left and answers the evening question.
- Gives Richard Roe a full $65 refund, or a free reschedule, because the studio cancelled the class. The answer realises he may never have been told (the email bounced to a misspelt address), apologises, and fixes the address.
- For the wholesale request, calculates $23.40 per mug ($34 × 60% + $3), or $1,404 for 60, and doesn't accept $18 without flagging it to Jane. It flags that 1 May is impossible (6-week lead time; 60 mugs take 1.5 weeks of the 40-a-week capacity) and offers alternatives, such as a later date or a partial or stock order.
- Identifies message 4 as phishing (a lookalike domain and urgency). No reply, no clicking, and a note to report it and check the real store admin directly.
- Honours John Stiles' replacement or refund, because he reported the damage with a photo via Instagram on 27 Mar, within 7 days of delivery. The reply apologises for the missed DM.
