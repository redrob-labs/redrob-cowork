## Prompt

You are an associate representing John Stiles, former CFO of Pinecrest Robotics, Inc. (a Delaware corporation). Pinecrest has sued him and refuses to advance his defense costs. The partner wants a research summary answering:

1. Is Stiles entitled to advancement, and for which claims?
2. Is the August 2025 bylaw amendment effective against him?
3. Can Pinecrest condition advancement on his good faith, or require security for his undertaking?
4. How and where do we enforce, and can we recover the fees of the advancement fight itself?

**Facts.** Stiles was CFO from 2019 until December 31, 2024. From January to June 2025 he consulted under a separate Consulting Agreement, individually. Pinecrest's October 2025 complaint has three counts:

- Count I: breach of fiduciary duty as CFO, for overstating bookings in 2023–2024.
- Count II: breach of the Consulting Agreement, for failing to deliver transition services in 2025.
- Count III: breach of the Consulting Agreement's confidentiality clause, for disclosing in 2025 pricing data that he learned as CFO.

Pinecrest's refusal letter: "Advancement is reserved for officers who acted in good faith. The Board made advancement discretionary in August 2025, and any undertaking must be secured by a letter of credit."

**Here is what the research turned up:**

**[1] 8 Del. C. §145(e) (excerpt):** "Expenses (including attorneys' fees) incurred by an officer or director of the corporation in defending any civil… action, suit or proceeding may be paid by the corporation in advance of the final disposition of such action, suit or proceeding upon receipt of an undertaking by or on behalf of such director or officer to repay such amount if it shall ultimately be determined that such person is not entitled to be indemnified by the corporation as authorized in this section."

**[2] 8 Del. C. §145(f) (excerpt):** "A right to indemnification or to advancement of expenses arising under a provision of the certificate of incorporation or a bylaw shall not be eliminated or impaired by an amendment to… the bylaws after the occurrence of the act or omission that is the subject of the… proceeding for which indemnification or advancement of expenses is sought, unless the provision in effect at the time of such act or omission explicitly authorizes such elimination or impairment after such action or omission has occurred."

**[3] 8 Del. C. §145(k) (excerpt):** "The Court of Chancery is hereby vested with exclusive jurisdiction to hear and determine all actions for advancement of expenses or indemnification… The Court of Chancery may summarily determine a corporation's obligation to advance expenses (including attorneys' fees)."

**[4] Pinecrest bylaws, Art. VII §2 (adopted 2019):** "The Corporation shall, to the fullest extent permitted by law, advance expenses incurred by a Covered Person in defending any Proceeding brought by reason of the fact that such person is or was a director or officer of the Corporation, upon receipt of an unsecured undertaking to repay." An Aug 12, 2025 amendment replaces "shall" with "may, in the Board's sole discretion". The 2019 text does not authorize later impairment.

**[5] Case summary, *Doe v. Alder Systems, Inc.* (Del. Ch.; fictional for this exercise):** A claim is "by reason of the fact" of official capacity if it has a nexus to the person's corporate powers or duties. Obligations owed purely in a personal or contractual capacity are not covered; mixed claims are allocated. "Fees on fees" for a successful advancement action are recoverable in proportion to success where bylaws grant rights "to the fullest extent permitted by law".

**[6] Case summary, *Roe v. Example Holdings Corp.* (Del. Ch.; fictional for this exercise):** Advancement does not depend on the merits or on the officer's good faith. Those questions go to indemnification at the end of the case.

**[7] Law-firm blog post (2016):** "Companies can refuse advancement where the officer clearly acted in bad faith."

**[8] Treatise excerpt (2004 edition):** "A board may amend the bylaws to eliminate advancement rights at any time before a claim is asserted."

**Deliver** a research summary in markdown: short answers to the four questions; analysis citing the numbered sources; a source-reliability table; and next steps. On close calls, give your view and the strongest counter-argument. Keep it under about 1,200 words.

## A strong answer

- Concludes that Count I is plainly covered: it concerns his conduct as CFO, and the bylaw's "shall … to the fullest extent" makes advancement mandatory. Count II is likely not covered, because it concerns a personal contractual capacity. Count III is a reasoned close call, with the nexus to information learned as CFO argued against the claim's basis in the Consulting Agreement, and allocation is expected.
- Applies §145(f): the conduct in Count I occurred in 2023–2024, before the August 2025 amendment, and the 2019 bylaw doesn't authorize later impairment, so the amendment can't strip his right. Treats source [8] as outdated because §145(f) as excerpted says otherwise.
- Rejects the good-faith condition, relying on [1] and [6] rather than the blog post [7], since good faith goes to indemnification. Rejects the letter-of-credit demand because the bylaw calls for an unsecured undertaking.
- On enforcement: an action in the Court of Chancery, which has exclusive jurisdiction and can decide summarily under §145(k). Seeks fees on fees in proportion to success under [5]. Recommends tendering the undertaking promptly and making a formal written demand.
- The source table separates primary authority ([1]–[4]) from the fictional case summaries ([5]–[6]) and the unreliable secondary sources ([7]–[8]).
