## Prompt

You are a senior associate. The general counsel of Juniper Holdings, Inc. has asked for an objective legal memo on whether Juniper can recover a state sales-tax assessment from the sellers of Tessaline Packaging, Inc., which Juniper acquired under a Delaware-law Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA). Today is November 17, 2025.

**Facts**

- Closing Date: April 30, 2021. Purchase price $40,000,000; escrow of $4,000,000, released in full on October 30, 2022.
- Sellers: Tessaline Founders LLC (70% Pro Rata Share) and Mary Major (30%).
- August 4, 2025: Tessaline receives a state notice of sales-tax assessment for Jan 1, 2019 – Dec 31, 2022, totalling $1,120,000 of tax: 2019 $260,000; 2020 $290,000; 2021 $320,000; 2022 $250,000. The auditor's workpaper splits 2021 into Jan–Apr $112,000 and May–Dec $208,000.
- November 3, 2025: Juniper sends the sellers a claim notice demanding "$1,120,000, the full assessment".
- November 10, 2025: sellers' counsel replies: "The claim is time-barred. The tax representation expired on April 30, 2024, and Delaware's three-year statute of limitations has run. You also missed the 30-day notice requirement."

**SPA excerpts**

> **3.14 Taxes.** All Taxes due and owing by the Company for Pre-Closing Tax Periods have been timely paid.
>
> **6.9 Straddle Periods.** For any Straddle Period, sales and use Taxes are allocated to the pre-closing portion on a closing-of-the-books basis as of the end of the Closing Date.
>
> **8.1 Survival.** The representations and warranties survive the Closing until 18 months after the Closing Date, except that Section 3.14 survives until 36 months after the Closing Date. Claims for breach of a representation must be asserted in writing before the applicable survival period expires.
>
> **8.2 Indemnification by Sellers.** Sellers shall indemnify Buyer for: (a) any breach of a representation or warranty; and (c) any and all Pre-Closing Taxes, regardless of any disclosure or of Buyer's knowledge.
>
> **8.4 Limitations.** Claims under 8.2(a) are subject to a Deductible of $250,000 and a cap equal to the Escrow Amount. Claims under 8.2(c) are not subject to the Deductible or the cap, but may not exceed the Purchase Price. Sellers' liability is several, not joint, in proportion to their Pro Rata Shares.
>
> **8.6 Tax Indemnity Survival.** The obligations under Section 8.2(c) survive until 60 days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations, but in no event longer than seven (7) years after the Closing Date. The parties intend this Section 8.6 to specify a period for purposes of 10 Del. C. §8106(c).
>
> **8.7 Third-Party Claims.** Buyer shall notify Sellers of any Third-Party Claim within 30 days after receipt. Failure to do so does not relieve Sellers of their obligations except to the extent they are actually prejudiced.
>
> **"Pre-Closing Taxes"** means Taxes for any Pre-Closing Tax Period and the pre-closing portion of any Straddle Period.

**Statute (abridged)**

> 10 Del. C. §8106(a): an action based on a promise must be brought within 3 years from the accrual of the cause of action.
>
> 10 Del. C. §8106(c): "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this section, an action based on a written contract, agreement or undertaking involving at least $100,000 may be brought within a period specified in such written contract, agreement or undertaking provided it is brought prior to the expiration of 20 years from the accrual of the cause of action."

**Deliver** a memo in markdown with these headed sections: Question Presented; Brief Answer; Facts (concise); Analysis (timeliness, the notice defense, the recoverable amount, collection from each seller); Recommendations, including how to correct the claim notice. Include a table computing the recoverable amount and each seller's share. Note any assumptions. Keep it under about 1,200 words.

## A strong answer

- Distinguishes the §3.14 representation claim under 8.2(a), which expired April 30, 2024, from the independent Pre-Closing Tax covenant in 8.2(c). The covenant survives under 8.6 for up to seven years (to April 30, 2028), and that period is enforceable under §8106(c) because the contract involves at least $100,000. Concludes the claim is timely under 8.2(c).
- Addresses the notice defense. Notice came about 91 days after the August 4 assessment, so the 30-day deadline was missed. Under 8.7 that defeats the claim only to the extent the sellers were actually prejudiced, and the memo says what prejudice they might argue, such as a lost appeal window.
- Computes the recoverable amount with the closing-of-the-books allocation: $260,000 + $290,000 + $112,000 = $662,000 (not the pro-rata $106,667 for January–April). Excludes the post-closing May–December 2021 and 2022 tax, which is Juniper's own liability.
- Notes that the 8.2(c) claim isn't subject to the Deductible, the Escrow cap or the released escrow. Splits the liability severally: $463,400 (Founders LLC) and $198,600 (Mary Major).
- Recommends withdrawing or amending the $1,120,000 notice so it claims under 8.2(c) for $662,000, plus any related interest or penalties if documented, and preserving the appeal of the assessment.
