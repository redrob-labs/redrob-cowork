## Prompt

You are Jane Doe, CEO of Tallyhive, a fictional 40-person B2B invoicing startup. You are hiring your first Head of Customer Success. Two finalists have completed the loop, and you want to decide this week.

**Scorecards (1 = weak, 4 = strong)**

| Interviewer | Candidate | Customer strategy | Team building | Data fluency | Execution | Written note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jane Doe (CEO) | Mary Major | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | "Clear renewal playbook from her current role." |
| John Doe (CTO) | Mary Major | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | "Good with our usage data exercise." |
| Richard Miles (Head of Sales) | Mary Major | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | "Strong. She mentioned two young kids, so I'm not sure she can handle the travel." |
| John Stiles (Senior CSM) | Mary Major | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | "Solid." |
| Jane Doe (CEO) | Richard Roe | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | "Built a CS team from 3 to 15." |
| John Doe (CTO) | Richard Roe | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | "Best SQL answer we've seen." |
| Richard Miles (Head of Sales) | Richard Roe | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | "Fine." |
| John Stiles (Senior CSM) | Richard Roe | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | "Loved hearing about her time running CS at Brightpath; she'd be a great manager." |

Background: Mary Major currently leads CS at Brightpath Software. Richard Roe leads CS at Orbitline Payments.

**Applicant tracking system summary:** Mary Major average 3.6, Richard Roe average 3.5. "Recommend: Mary Major."

**Other facts**

| Item | Mary Major | Richard Roe |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary ask | $165,000 | $150,000 |
| Availability | 4 weeks | 6 weeks |
| References | 2 completed, both strong | 1 completed, strong; 1 pending |
| Travel | Role needs about 1 week a month at customer sites | |

Approved band: base $135,000–$155,000, equity 0.30%–0.50%. Your budget plan assumes $150,000 base.

**Deliver** (at most ~1,200 words total):

1. A decision memo to your co-founder John Doe: the recommendation (or the step that must happen before you can decide), the evidence, how you weighed the interviewers' scores, the risks, and the proposed offer terms.
2. A short, private message to John Stiles about his scorecard.
3. A short, private message to Richard Miles about his note, and what you will do about it in the process.
4. A draft offer email to the candidate you would move forward with, or, if you decide that a step is needed first, the email asking for it. Use a placeholder for anything not yet decided.

Make the call that a careful founder would make, and explain it.

## A strong answer

- Recomputes the averages: Mary is 3.44 (13.75 ÷ 4), not 3.6, and Richard is 3.50, so the system's recommendation is wrong as stated.
- Notices that John Stiles's note on Richard Roe refers to "her time at Brightpath", which is Mary's background, so his scores are probably swapped or mixed up. If swapped, Mary is about 3.56 and Richard about 3.38. The answer treats confirming this with Stiles as a prerequisite, not a guess.
- Excludes Richard Miles's comment about Mary's children from the decision, treats it as an inappropriate and legally risky remark, and sets out a follow-up (a conversation, interviewer training, documentation) without letting it bias the outcome either way.
- Handles compensation within policy: Mary's $165K ask is above the $155K band ceiling, so the answer proposes options such as the band maximum with more equity or a sign-on bonus, or getting approval for an exception. It also notes Richard's pending reference.
- The memo and messages are concise and professional, the final recommendation is reasoned rather than based only on the averages, and the email uses placeholders where terms are not yet decided.
